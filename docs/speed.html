End-to-end has two honest rungs. The FLOOR is per-message sealing (one AEAD seal per frame): the per-call overhead dominates a tiny frame, so it sits near 400 to 700 thousand frames/s and cannot amortize. The CEILING is the same AEAD when messages are framed into a batch (a shm ring write, a jumbo frame, an at-rest recording), which amortizes that overhead to the AES-NI bulk rate: tens of millions of frames/s, peaking at 210,579,134 on this host. Each cell's ceiling uses only the batch that is structurally realizable for its transport and seal mode, so a per-message wire route honestly stays at its floor.

Why end-to-end has a floor and a ceiling

A 77-byte frame under AES-256-GCM, measured on this host. Per-message sealing cannot amortize the per-call cost; framing many messages into one sealed unit climbs to the bulk cipher rate. Batch buys throughput and spends latency: the fill latency is the wait to accumulate a batch at your arrival rate (batch / rate), plus the fixed cost to seal it. Pick the smallest batch that meets your required rate within your latency budget.

Run it on your machine

Every number here is measured. Measure your own box the same way. The probe autodetects your hardware and toolchains, runs what it can, and writes a result file. It needs only Python 3, no robobus install, and it uses your real crypto library and liboqs when present. Works on a Windows workstation, a Linux server, a Raspberry Pi, or a Chromebook's Linux container.

1. Measure

On any machine you own:

python3 bench/probe.py

It prints your codec, ring, latency and post-quantum seal numbers and writes probe-<os>-<arch>.json. Missing a metric? It tells you exactly what to install (cryptography, liboqs-python, or the native ring).

Two regimes: small-frame rate, and bulk bandwidth

A bus has two ceilings, and robobus is measured at both. At tiny frames the limit is FRAME RATE (overhead-bound): the shm ring moves about 74 million 75-byte frames/s, roughly 5.6 GB/s. At large frames the limit is BANDWIDTH (memory-bound): the same ring streams at 52 GB/s zero-copy, against a raw-memcpy silicon ceiling near 82 GB/s. Both are the true maximum on this machine, not a portable floor.

The seal ceiling has three rungs (AES-256-GCM, CNSA 2.0)

Sealing is not one number. Sealing each wire PDU on its own is the FLOOR; framing many messages into one sealed buffer amortizes the per-call cost to the AES-NI bulk rate; running that on every core is the machine's aggregate ceiling. All three measured here, kept separate.

per-message (wire PDU floor)
native 77 M f/s

one C AES-256-GCM seal per frame, ~13 ns. Each UDP/TCP/DDS frame sealed on its own.

batched, single core
native 211 M f/s

many frames sealed as one buffer (shm ring / recording / jumbo), per-call setup amortized.

aggregate, all cores
native 828 M f/s

the batched seal on all 10 cores (21.52 GB/s, 4.09x single core). The machine's true seal throughput.
