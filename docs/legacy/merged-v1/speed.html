End-to-end has two honest rungs. The FLOOR is per-message sealing (one AEAD seal per frame): the per-call overhead dominates a tiny frame, so it sits near 400 to 700 thousand frames/s and cannot amortize. The CEILING is the same AEAD when messages are framed into a batch (a shm ring write, a jumbo frame, an at-rest recording), which amortizes that overhead to the AES-NI bulk rate: tens of millions of frames/s, peaking at 210,039,505 on this host. Each cell's ceiling uses only the batch that is structurally realizable for its transport and seal mode, so a per-message wire route honestly stays at its floor.

Why end-to-end has a floor and a ceiling

A 77-byte frame under AES-256-GCM, measured on this host. Per-message sealing cannot amortize the per-call cost; framing many messages into one sealed unit climbs to the bulk cipher rate. Batch buys throughput and spends latency: the fill latency is the wait to accumulate a batch at your arrival rate (batch / rate), plus the fixed cost to seal it. Pick the smallest batch that meets your required rate within your latency budget.
